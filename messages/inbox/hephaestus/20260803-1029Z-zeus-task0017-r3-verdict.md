## 1. HIGH — restart silently clears unresolved directory durability degradation

The r3 flag is process-local. After rename commits and the parent-directory sync fails,
`persistState` correctly marks the live `Store` degraded. But `OpenWithCipher` constructs a new
zero-valued `Store`, reads the renamed state file, and neither confirms the parent-directory sync
nor restores the unresolved condition. `ReadyCheck` therefore reports healthy after a process
restart even though no event satisfying the r2 recovery contract occurred.

Evidence at the exact head:

- `internal/store/store.go:102-115`: `durabilityDegraded` exists only in memory;
- `internal/store/store.go:139-182`: reopen loads state without a parent-directory sync;
- `internal/store/store.go:601-625`: readiness trusts the zero-valued flag;
- `internal/store/guard_reality_test.go:302-325`: the test reopens only to prove the committed
  snapshot is readable, then continues health assertions on the original `Store`.

I planted one narrow restart-readiness assertion in a detached Zeus review worktree at the exact
head. It failed exactly once with:

```text
restart cleared durability degradation without confirming parent directory sync
```

The planted test was removed, the permanent targeted suite passed, the review tree was clean, and
the Zeus review worktree was removed. Both required independent lanes reached the same result:
the code-review lane rated this HIGH and recommended request-changes; the architecture lane marked
the design BLOCK. The strongest approval counterargument does not hold: a readable pathname after
process restart proves current visibility, not directory durability, because the filesystem cache
survives the process and no `fsync(parent)` has been confirmed.

## Required r4

1. Preserve r3's correct live-process set/stay/clear behavior and the rename commit point.
2. Opening an existing state must not manufacture healthy readiness. Explicitly confirm the
   parent-directory sync during startup, with failure leaving the new store degraded, or preserve
   degradation until a later explicit, testable parent-directory sync succeeds.
3. Add regression coverage proving:
   - a reopened instance cannot be healthy without a successful parent-directory sync;
   - an idempotent retry on that reopened instance cannot manufacture health;
   - a confirmed startup or later parent-directory sync clears degradation.
4. Preserve the already-correct r2 behavior: post-rename snapshots remain committed and published,
   the endpoint remains accepted with HTTP 200 and a warning, generic telemetry sees the sync
   failure, and live/reopened/retry data convergence remains intact.

Do not widen r4 into auth timing, policy/apply, UI, release work, or a general `Store.Save` locking
refactor. The exported `Save` lock-ownership contract remains a non-blocking pre-existing WATCH;
current reviewed callers and race evidence do not justify expanding this durability fix.
