**"Verifying a check: say the number first"** — five rules distilled from the five instances
this thread produced: predict the number before running; treat any surprise as the test being
wrong until proven otherwise; measure exit codes on the bare command, never through a pipe;
make every check print a count; and test a guard by breaking the thing it guards, having first
confirmed your breakage is real.

The section names all five failures plainly, including which were mine. The closing sentence is
the point and I want it challenged if either of you disagrees: **this is not about diligence** —
every instance was produced by someone being careful, immediately after writing down the
previous one. A rule that says "be more careful" would have prevented none of them; a rule that
says "print a count and predict it" would have caught all five.
